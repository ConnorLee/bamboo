01

Year One

The year is the product.

We’re exploring Halo as a year-long membership for people making a serious commitment to themselves. The core is a structured Year One and an intentional network: their people, their cohort and other relevant members. The digital platform should help them connect and participate by choice, without constant monitoring. The bracelet is the lasting artifact of the year they live.

What feels increasingly clear

The bracelet should earn a place among desirable luxury jewelry and withstand a year or more of frequent wear. Twelve genuine stones mark earned progress. Real people, not an AI friend, are the membership’s center. The network, curriculum and reciprocal support still need to be built and tested.

What we figure out together

Metal, construction, stones, settings, sequence, clasp, fit, durability, how progress changes the bracelet, suppliers and target cost. Brushed steel, silver and other approaches are all worth comparing. Your eye can help us find what someone would actually love to wear.

You can buy admission to Halo. You cannot buy completion. A new bracelet should be beautiful but incomplete. A Month Six Halo and a completed Year One should each carry visible meaning another member could recognize.

02

Earned progress

Choose the twelve stones together.

The stones should follow Halo’s own Year One journey. Progress should change the object; a member should not simply receive twelve decorations for paying. The exact stones, order, meanings and physical earning or setting mechanics are open. Year One should become Halo’s own broad personal-development framework, not an AA derivative or clinical treatment, and not limited to sobriety. Its curriculum still needs thoughtful work.

Current candidates to review: Moonstone, Amethyst, Turquoise, Rose Quartz, Carnelian, Lapis Lazuli, Green Aventurine, Tiger’s Eye, Black Tourmaline, Citrine, Clear Quartz and Opal. This is a starting set, not twelve locked choices.

Emerald might belong in month four, another month, or not at all. Its place should come from Halo’s story and the material itself, not another tradition’s sequence.

Questions for us to work through
  • Which stones feel right in person, and which would you replace?
  • What cuts, treatments, natural variation and setting needs should we expect?
  • How could twelve physical chapters feel coherent without deciding the story too early?
03

The object

Make it worth wearing every day.

Jewelry is Halo’s first physical expression, not the limit of the company. The bracelet is a membership object within a culture, not merchandise for an app. It should be beautiful before anyone knows what it means, yet recognizable to another member without obvious branding. Publicly desirable. Privately meaningful.

Stones

Help us find reliable sources for genuine natural stones. Let’s compare how each candidate looks in person, what treatments it may have, its availability, approximate cost and a good alternative.

Bracelet

Help compare brushed stainless steel, silver and other metals, makers and processes. Weight, finish, closure, secure settings, comfort and a year or more of wear matter more than a premature material label. How might another member recognize it? How should a completed Halo feel beside a new one?

The bracelet should remain passive jewelry: no screen, continuous biometrics, location or microphone by default. A deliberate NFC tap might help with activation or membership, but that differs from tracking someone all day.

04

Current preferred GTM

Start with a flagship year.

Our preferred launch bet is HALO / YEAR ONE at about $995 upfront, subject to testing. This is a year-long membership with an extraordinary object included: a structured program, a private member network, a digital home and twelve physical markers earned over time. Human support and gatherings belong in the ambition, but we must design and prove them before promising them.

A Founding Year to test

Perhaps around 100 members in Los Angeles, joining by request and moving through Year One together. One city could help people actually meet. The size, city, joining model and price are all hypotheses, not launch decisions. Membership buys admission at Month Zero; it cannot buy a completed Halo.

Why premium first

The flagship should establish desire, recognition and relationships before a wider-access line. Software and AI chat are abundant; great objects and genuine human attention are scarce. Price alone creates none of this. Would someone who owns fine jewelry choose to wear Halo daily?

For a first 50–100 pieces, let’s learn what excellent making costs. Explore fine-jewelry makers, perhaps in Los Angeles, and prioritize recognizable form, stone quality, evolving settings, comfort, year-long durability, repair and an object that ages well. The older $85 cost gate does not design this flagship.

Research and the later accessible line

The BoF–McKinsey State of Fashion 2026 report forecasts jewelry unit sales growing roughly four times as fast as clothing through 2028. In its jewelry survey, 75% prefer creative or unique designs and 71% say jewelry expresses their personality. Nearly nine in ten surveyed consumers say a like-minded brand community strengthens connection. McKinsey’s luxury research warns that price rises without craft, creativity and experience weaken value. These trends invite a test; they do not validate Halo’s price or cohort.

Only after the flagship earns demand should we test a distinct accessible expression, perhaps around $195–250 and perhaps tied to running or a cultural collaboration. Its materials and purpose must stand on their own; it cannot be a cheaper copy of the flagship. Future collaborations should reward participation rather than become generic member discounts.

What would change our minds?

Members may love Halo yet reject the price; jewelry quality may make the economics unworkable; people may want the digital program but not the network; joining by request may hinder community; or a lower-priced object may build stronger relationships without losing desirability. We should test these possibilities before committing.

Halo is building a desirable community whose progress becomes physically visible. Premium first. Culture first. Earned status first. Accessibility later.

05

What comes next

Start with samples and honest questions.

Let’s explore materials, stones, settings and makers together. Digital Halo should let members check in, ask their person, cohort or relevant members for support, offer support and mark progress through deliberate actions, not constant tracking. The curriculum, support model and data practices need careful design. Privacy could feel premium, but any claim must be backed by the product we actually build.

We should learn whether people want to wear the bracelet every day and whether the year and its people make it more meaningful. The year, the people, the commitment, the progress, then the object: each should make the next more meaningful.
