01

Preferred launch hypothesis

Premium Year One

Test a luxury-priced, year-long membership at approximately $995 upfront. The proposed offer includes a structured program, a private member network, a digital home and a bracelet completed with twelve natural stones earned over the year. A first cohort of roughly 100 members in Los Angeles, joining by request, would test whether local participation becomes part of the value. The program, support and gatherings must be designed before they are promised.

Lifestyle and jewelry

Test Halo as a premium lifestyle membership with a luxury bracelet people want to wear on its own. The object needs a recognizable form, strong materials, finish, settings, packaging and a repair path. The year and member network give it further meaning; price alone does not make it luxury.

Validation

Test how people categorize Halo, whether they want to wear the bracelet daily, and whether the year adds value. Compare reservations, acquisition cost, contribution margin, gifting intent, brand perception, member participation, interest in local gatherings and Year Two. Validate the cohort model and jewelry economics before setting a launch price.

The current public offer remains $189. This premium concept has not replaced it. A separate accessible line around $195–250 may be explored after the flagship demonstrates demand; it would need its own design and economics.

Market research behind the hypothesis

The BoF–McKinsey State of Fashion 2026 reports interest in distinctive jewelry and community. McKinsey’s luxury research stresses craft, creativity and experience alongside price. These are market signals, not validation of Halo’s offer.

02

Membership

Year One experience

Year One combines a personal-development program, member participation and earned physical progress. The curriculum and community model are still to be defined. Members should choose when to check in, connect or seek support; the bracelet remains passive jewelry without continuous tracking. Halo is not a clinical or sobriety-specific program.

A new bracelet starts incomplete. One natural stone is earned each month, and a completed bracelet marks a finished Year One. The physical method of adding stones and the rules for earning them need further work.

03

Jewelry and sourcing

Bracelet requirements

The approved Year One imagery sets the current visual direction; it does not qualify production materials. The bracelet should be desirable as jewelry, comfortable for daily wear and durable for at least a year. Compare brushed stainless steel, silver and other viable metals before committing to a material or cost target.

Construction

Assess weight, finish, clasp, fit, secure settings, stone installation, repairability and manufacturing process. Make samples and wear-test them before finalizing the design.

Stone supply

Source genuine natural stones. Compare appearance, treatments, variation, availability, durability, cost and suitable alternatives. Supplier quotes and production yield are still unknown.

04

Earned progression

Twelve stones

The approved Year One direction uses twelve genuine natural stones, one earned per month. Opal is Month 12. The other stones and their meanings can receive minor sourcing or bill-of-materials adjustments without reopening the approved imagery. The final sequence and setting process require a qualified supplier and a coherent Year One curriculum.

Current visualized stone palette

Moonstone, Amethyst, Turquoise, Rose Quartz, Carnelian, Lapis Lazuli, Green Aventurine, Tiger’s Eye, Black Tourmaline, Citrine, Clear Quartz and Opal. This is a sourcing reference, not a set of approved supplier specifications.
