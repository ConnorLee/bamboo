02 / THE MECHANICAL OBJECT

Less inside. More meaning.

Designed to be prototyped.

A 5.5 mm stone in a 6.6 mm carrier is the starting envelope. The receiver uses opposed lugs and a short bayonet turn, with a positive restraint against accidental reverse rotation.

The click confirms seating; it is not the only thing holding the stone. A jeweler-serviceable release and a separate receiver insert keep the mechanism plausible to machine and repair.

8 mm band · 4.6 mm maximum assembly target · dimensions unvalidated. No water-resistance, skin-safety or retention claim before testing.

03 / THE YEAR CASE

Twelve small moments.
One lasting object.

A keepsake drawer cabinet with quiet numbers and a soft tactile reveal. Something that belongs in a home for a year.

Open only this chapter.

Each drawer has a message, a lifted natural gemstone, a concealed scan point and another line of copy beneath the stone.

The drawer pulls clear of its neighbors for a calm reveal and an isolated NFC read. A woven ribbon lifts the module without prying against the stone.

No lock polices the sequence. The app records earned milestones; the bracelet remains a personal artifact.

Other structures considered

Nested trays slow access to later chapters. Twelve hinged mini-boxes add volume and parts. A sliding tray with individual lids is the simpler cost alternative. Concealed paperboard magnets may close lids, but must sit away from antennas; magnets never retain bracelet modules.

04 / THE INVISIBLE CONNECTION

Bring your phone
to your stone.

It feels like activating the gemstone. The iPhone reads the thin NFC inlay concealed immediately behind it in the drawer.

The preferred inlay sits just behind the metal carrier in a nonmetallic nest. A directly-under-stone variant needs read testing. Compatible iPhone background reading presents a notification that the user taps to open Halo; a foreground scan is the fallback. Neither path is implemented by this browser study.
